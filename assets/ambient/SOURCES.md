# Ambient loops: sources and licences

Nine 120 s stereo AAC loops (44.1 kHz, about 96 kb/s) that play softly under
recitation videos. Regenerate all of them with `node scripts/make-ambient.mjs`
(no network needed; it uses the clips in `source/`).

| id | made how | third-party material |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `rain` | synthesized (ffmpeg, seeded noise) | none |
| `rain-thunder` | synthesized rain bed (same recipe as `rain`) plus four distant-thunder rolls cut from a recording | thunder: recording 1 below |
| `rain-window` | synthesized | none |
| `stream` | synthesized | none |
| `campfire` | synthesized | none |
| `crickets` | recording, filtered and layered | recording 2 below |
| `wind` | synthesized | none |
| `ocean` | synthesized | none |
| `night-hum` | synthesized | none |

## Synthesized files

`rain`, `rain-window`, `stream`, `campfire`, `wind`, `ocean`, `night-hum` and
the rain bed of `rain-thunder` are original work generated by
`scripts/make-ambient.mjs` from ffmpeg's seeded noise generators and filters.
They contain no third-party material and carry no third-party rights.

## What I could not verify, and what I rejected

- I could not listen to any file. All checks were numeric (levels, spectra,
  loop seam metrics) and visual (spectrograms). Whether the loops sound good,
  and whether the recordings hold anything unwanted, is unchecked by ear.
- Cricket clip. The licence is as stated by the uploader on OpenGameArt; I could
  not independently confirm that syncopika is the person who recorded it, only
  that the page says so and offers it under CC0. The raw recording is lo-fi. Its
  spectrogram shows the cricket chirps at about 2.9 to 3.9 kHz (one pulse every
  0.35 s or so), low-frequency thumps, and recurring events of a couple of
  seconds each: a steady tone near 2.2 kHz together with a diffuse cloud around
  5 kHz. I could not identify the source of those events (another insect is
  plausible; a bird or frog cannot be excluded without listening). The filter
  chain removes everything below about 2.8 kHz (the 2.2 kHz tone is notched and
  sits roughly 30 dB under the chirps after filtering) and everything above about
  4.3 kHz, but I cannot rule out that something other than crickets remains
  inside the 2.9 to 4.0 kHz pass band. Please listen to `crickets.m4a` once.
- Thunder clip. The page describes thunder recorded in Finland and the
  spectrogram shows broadband low-frequency rumble; only content below about
  230 Hz is used. The 32 s clip also shows steady energy below about 60 Hz that
  may be wind or handling noise rather than thunder.
- Rejected after reading the pages: `File:Storm thunderbolts.ogg` (public domain
  via pdsounds.org, but uploaded by a third party "in his name", so not
  first-hand); the Internet Archive item "Nighttime Bug - Field Cricket
  Chirping" (Public Domain Mark, but uploaded by a company account rather than
  the named recordist); all other Commons thunder and cricket files I checked
  (CC BY, CC BY-SA). Pixabay, YouTube and anything under BY/SA/NC/ND terms were
  not used.
